# Cove pitch display model

This is a **nonfunctional, early internal-mass concept prop** for explaining wave-relative motion. The current Cove-S simulation and CAD instead depict a surface body and submerged reaction body with a power takeoff between them. Do not present this print as a scale model of that architecture or as evidence of electrical generation, watertightness, or autonomous AUV docking.

## Files and printing

- `Cove_shell_section_print_twice.stl`: print **two**; each section is 110 mm across and 85 mm tall, with a front cutaway.
- `Cove_end_cap_print_twice.stl`: print **two**; each cap is 110 mm across and 5 mm thick, with a 6.6 mm center hole.
- `Cove_pitch_model_preview.png`: assembly concept illustration, not a validated engineering drawing.

Suggested first slicer settings: PLA, 0.24–0.28 mm layers, three walls, 10–15% infill, no supports. Lay caps flat and print shell sections upright. Check the slicer preview and fit to your own printer before committing to a long print.

## Assemble

1. Glue the two shell sections end to end, aligning the openings. Glue on the bottom cap.
2. Place a smooth 6 mm rod or dowel about 180 mm long through the cap centers, fixed to the bottom cap.
3. Add a compression spring, a visible stack of M6 fender washers, and a second spring. Choose springs with an inside diameter over 6 mm. Check for roughly 20–40 mm of smooth travel.
4. Add the upper cap after confirming that the washer stack moves freely when the shell is moved vertically.
5. Make a visual AUV and cradle from a marker body, printed shape, pipe section, or foamboard. Mount the cradle below the shell on a rigid stand. It is a **dock mockup**, not a functioning charger.

Photograph the complete display, the cutaway, and a person holding it for scale. A short motion video can be captioned: **“Inertial-motion illustration; electrical output not tested.”**

If printing slips, a clear 100–120 mm plastic container, cardboard caps, and the same rod/washer/spring arrangement can serve as a fallback. The motion and honest labeling matter more than the finish.
